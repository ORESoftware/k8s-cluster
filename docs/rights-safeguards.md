# Rights and safeguards red lines

Status: **requirements baseline pending independent review**  
Tracks: DEN-935  
Legal feasibility dependency: DEN-934

These safeguards apply before any model, optimizer, dashboard, notification, assignment mechanism, or policy update is considered for live use.

## Rights that optimization cannot waive

Every affected person must receive understandable notice of the process, material deadlines, data use, automated assistance, consequences, and available review. The system must preserve meaningful opportunities to respond, correct records, present evidence, obtain representation where applicable, request human review, and use an independent appeal, complaint, or ombuds route without retaliation.

Accessibility is a hard precondition, not a best-effort objective. Registered actions must account for language access, disability accommodation, low-bandwidth or offline use, and a non-digital fallback. A failed accessibility or delivery path must stop or route to a human process rather than silently reducing participation.

## Prohibited and restricted inference

The action registry prohibits legal-merits and credibility variables, protected traits and proxies, political timing, publicity salience, verdict/remedy objectives, and learned governance weights. Biometric, emotion, credibility, ideology, or protected-trait inference is excluded absent a separately established lawful, scientifically valid, reviewed basis and a new registry version.

No reward or throughput metric can override notice, response, representation, discovery, appeal, recusal, conflict, quorum, dissent, accessibility, privacy, or human-control requirements.

## Data and evidence controls

- Collect only data needed for a registered administrative purpose.
- Record purpose, provenance, chain of custody, quality limitations, retention, sealing, redaction, and access authority.
- Preserve immutable historical events while propagating corrections into current beliefs, schedules, eligibility, reports, and future training datasets.
- Expose the origin and correction history of data used by an administrative action.
- Prevent confidential, sealed, witness-protection, or security-sensitive information from entering public decision records.
- Provide documented breach response, deletion, and access/correction workflows where legally applicable.

## Human control and contestability

High-risk or irreversible actions require named human approvals. Decision records must explain whether automation affected the action, what registered observations were used, what constraints passed, which approvals were present, and how to contest the result. Missing approvals, unknown actions, unknown features, or failed preconditions deny.

The manual alternative must be operationally usable. A kill switch must stop new automated proposals without erasing immutable history or preventing human service delivery.

## Required scenario suite

Before a public pilot, end-to-end tests must cover represented and unrepresented people; non-English users; disabled users; remote and low-connectivity users; data corrections; missed or failed notices; recusal and conflicts; emergency requests; appeal; sealed evidence; retaliation concerns; human override; model outage; rollback; and false-positive proxy/adverse-impact alerts.

Adverse-impact results must include uncertainty, sample-size limitations, subgroup and intersectional analysis, counterfactual checks where valid, and a prohibition on claiming fairness from underpowered data.

## Review gate

Independent legal, privacy, accessibility, security, human-rights, and community reviewers must approve or explicitly reject each requirement before any public pilot. Their annotations belong in policy sources and review records, not hidden in model code.
